Green Coffee in Nepal

From the eastern hills of Ilam to your cup — how we grow, process, and source with intention.

Green Coffee

Green coffee in Nepal.

Green coffee is the raw, unroasted seed straight from the ripe cherry — before it's ever touched by heat. In Nepal, it almost always starts the same way: on a small hillside farm, hand-picked cherry by cherry, often growing in the shade of other trees rather than in open, cleared fields.

That farming style is shaped as much by geography as by choice. Nepal's coffee-growing hills sit between subtropical lowlands and cooler highland air, and most farmers work small plots without heavy fertilizers or chemical inputs — less a certification strategy, more simply how farming has always been done there. The result is a coffee sector that's naturally close to organic, and farms that double as genuine biodiversity, supporting birds and other wildlife alongside the crop itself.

Nepal's coffee industry is still young, and national production remains modest — a few hundred tonnes a year across the whole country. That makes Nepali coffee genuinely rare on the world market, especially next to long-established origins. But that scarcity is increasingly working in its favor: as specialty roasters look past saturated, well-known origins for something more traceable and distinctive, interest in Nepali coffee has been steadily climbing, both from international buyers and from a growing domestic café culture in cities like Kathmandu and Pokhara.

Right now, the sector sits at an inflection point — small-scale but improving quickly, with more producers experimenting with processing methods, investing in better infrastructure, and building the kind of reputation that takes an origin from "unknown" to "sought-after." It's a story still being written, and every farm contributing to it, including ours, is part of shaping where Nepali coffee goes next.

Our Coffee Farm

Grown in the hills, shaped with intention.

Our farm sits in the lower Ilam region, near the Ilam–Jhapa transition belt — a microclimate blending Terai warmth with hill elevation for balanced Arabica growing conditions. The cultivated area is planned for long-term soil health rather than maximum yield, with coffee as our primary crop grown in the shade of banana and avocado trees, enriched through organic composting, and carefully spaced and pruned for plant health.

How We Process

Processing Methods

At Ouz Coffee & Co., processing is where we shape the final character of our coffee. We apply controlled and intentional methods to highlight sweetness, clarity, and unique flavor profiles.

01

Natural Process (Sun-Dried Cherry)

Fully ripe cherries are carefully handpicked and dried on raised beds under the sun. This slow drying process allows the beans to absorb sugars from the fruit, resulting in a cup profile that is fruity, sweet, and full-bodied.

02

Washed Process (Clean & Bright)

Selected cherries are pulped and fermented to remove mucilage before being washed and dried. This method enhances clarity, brightness, and crisp acidity, showcasing the true origin character.

03

Anaerobic & Experimental Lots

For select micro-lots, we explore controlled fermentation techniques, including anaerobic processing. Cherries are fermented in sealed environments to develop complex, wine-like, and expressive flavor notes. These lots are produced in limited quantities with careful monitoring.
